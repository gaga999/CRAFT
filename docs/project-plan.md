# CRAFT Research Plan

This document describes the research scope, hypotheses, candidate architectures, representation objectives, and experimental comparisons. The methods below are proposals. Their empirical effectiveness and novelty remain to be established.

## 1. Research goal and scope

### Motivation

Contrastive reinforcement learning (CRL) learns relationships between state-action pairs and future goals. With action chunking, its critic conditions on a sequence of actions and scores a goal through learned representations:

$$
A_H=(a_t,\ldots,a_{t+H-1}),\qquad
f(s_t,A_H,g)=\phi(s_t,A_H)^\top\psi(g).
$$

The reference paper reports a policy extraction bottleneck in a controlled noisy manipulation experiment. Critic validation discrimination improves up to a relatively long chunk length, while policy success peaks at a shorter chunk length and then deteriorates. This occurs even with replanning after every executed action. The validation improvement is not monotonic across all lengths, and this observation must be checked in the selected experimental setting before generalizing it.

Decoupled Q-Chunking (DQC) transfers scores from a long-chunk critic to a shorter-chunk critic and extracts a short-chunk policy. CRAFT investigates generating effective long chunks by giving the actor direct access to learned representation structure. The critic score can remain a policy improvement signal alongside these additional representation pathways. [Three Steps at a Time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30640)

### Central research question

**How can CRL representations directly support action chunk generation, and how should representation learning change to make those representations useful for generation?**

The leading approach is to generate actions through a latent space informed by CRL. A complementary approach supplies learned embeddings as actor conditions. These approaches can be combined in one policy. Both require a concrete connection between the representation used by CRL and the information consumed or generated by the actor.

Success means improving downstream goal-reaching behavior under controlled comparisons. Lower representation loss, better critic discrimination, or accurate action reconstruction alone is insufficient to establish that success.

### Research priorities

| Direction | Role and intended contribution |
| --- | --- |
| CRL-informed latent action generation | Primary direction. Generate action-related latents whose structure is learned using CRL, then map them to executable action chunks. |
| Embedding-conditioned generation | Complementary direction. Expose CRL representations directly to the actor as conditioning information, potentially alongside raw observations and goals. |
| Generated subgoal conditioning | More complex extension. Generate an intended future-state embedding and condition action generation on it. |
| Action-related auxiliary objectives, including JEPA-style prediction | Shape the representation used by the primary generation pathway. Each objective needs an explicit mechanism and a downstream ablation. |
| Positive-only representation learning | Exploratory branch. Change how positive relationships are learned and study the resulting representation and control behavior. |
| Inference-time critic steering | Secondary branch. Integrate critic-based action refinement or guidance with the generative policy. |

Reconstruction is a possible training signal, feasibility diagnostic, and control experiment. The scientific focus is the relationship between CRL representation learning and action generation. Latent dimensionality reduction is a candidate mechanism, rather than a requirement or an assumed benefit.

The initial experimental scope is offline, state-based, simulated goal-conditioned control with robotic manipulation as the main application. OGBench and the available CRL+AC implementation provide a starting point. Generic scaling, world modeling as the main contribution, sim-to-real transfer, and broad adaptation are outside the primary scope. Action-conditioned prediction may still serve as an auxiliary representation objective.

### Position relative to existing work

Latent action policies, latent chunk generation, and representation-conditioned control already exist. The proposed contribution must be established through the specific CRL representation pathway, the generation problem it addresses, and controlled evidence of improvement. Relevant comparisons include latent action policies, latent chunk models, and CRL embedding-conditioned actors. Subgoal generation also needs comparison with hierarchical goal-conditioned methods. [PLAS](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v155/zhou21b.html), [Learning High-Frequency Continuous Action Chunks in Latent Space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24931), [Direction-Conditioned Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05087), [HIQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11949)

## 2. Research hypotheses and success criteria

| Hypothesis | Proposed mechanism | Evidence needed and possible failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1. CRL-informed latent generation improves policy extraction for long action chunks. | A representation organized around future outcomes may expose useful action structure to the generator. | Compare with direct action generation and a comparable latent generator without CRL representation supervision. Failure includes loss of action information, difficult latent sampling, or no downstream gain. |
| H2. CRL embedding conditioning improves action generation. | The actor can use learned goal-related information during generation. | Compare raw-input and embedding-conditioned policies under matched critic feedback and capacity. The actor may ignore the embedding or gain only from additional parameters. |
| H3. Action-related auxiliary objectives improve the representation's usefulness for generation. | Predicting action consequences or preserving executable action information may align the representation with the actor's needs. | Add objectives individually and measure policy success as well as representation diagnostics. Prediction or reconstruction can improve while policy performance deteriorates. |
| H4. Latent generation and embedding conditioning can be complementary. | The generated variable and the conditioning interface can provide different useful information. | Compare each pathway alone and their combination. Redundant information or interacting optimization errors may eliminate any additional gain. |

Primary outcomes are task success and performance as action chunk length changes. Costs and variability must accompany performance claims. More reliable multi-step execution would support an additional temporal abstraction benefit. Generating a long chunk while executing one action establishes a different result from successfully committing to that chunk for multiple steps.

## 3. Candidate architectures and data flow

### Shared notation and representation interfaces

Let \(s\) denote the current observation, \(g\) the desired goal, and \(A_H\) a dataset action chunk. Distinguish three interfaces:

- \(z_A\) is an action-related latent used to generate a chunk.
- \(h_g=\psi(g)\) is a goal embedding available before action generation.
- \(z_{\mathrm{sub}}\) is an embedding of an intended intermediate outcome.

A possible factorization of the state-action encoder is

$$
h=E_\eta(s,A_H),\qquad
\phi(s,A_H)=C_\omega(h),\qquad
z_A=R_\rho(h).
$$

Here \(C\) is a contrastive projection head and \(R\) defines the representation exposed to action generation. Candidates include using \(h\) directly, applying a separate action projection, or using the final contrastive embedding \(\phi\). These choices affect both information preservation and gradient sharing. A separate head can reduce direct objective conflict, but does not guarantee that the actor's latent retains useful CRL structure. The shared backbone and each head's gradient paths must be specified and tested.

The encoder \(E(s,A_H)\) requires a known action chunk. At training time it can supply a target representation. At deployment a generator must produce the latent from available inputs. A current-state embedding also needs a defined encoder or input view. Evaluating \(\psi(s)\) requires compatible state and goal inputs.

### A. Generate a CRL-informed action latent

For a dataset example, construct \(z_A=R(E(s,A_H))\). A conditional generator learns to produce this representation:

$$
\epsilon\sim p_0,\qquad
\hat z_A=G_\theta(\epsilon\mid c(s,g)),\qquad
\hat A_H=D_\delta(s,\hat z_A).
$$

The conditioning function \(c\) may use raw state and goal inputs, learned embeddings, or both. Flow matching and diffusion are candidate generators. The action head \(D\) must map generated latents to valid action chunks, with action bounds handled consistently with the baseline.

Training requires a defined latent target and a mechanism for learning the action mapping, such as reconstruction or supervised action generation. A conditional behavior-generation loss alone learns dataset behavior. Policy improvement through critic feedback, weighting, or distillation needs a separately specified objective. If retaining FQL extraction, document the flow teacher, one-step student, their sampling procedures, and where decoding occurs. [Flow Q-Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02538)

For fixed-target experiments, freeze the representation while training the generator and action head. Joint training is another candidate, but it creates moving latent targets. Any joint variant must define which losses update the encoder, how generation targets are detached or stabilized, and whether the decoder changes during actor improvement. Freezing decoder parameters can still permit gradients through its latent input.

At inference, the policy uses current observations, the goal, and sampled noise. Dataset actions and future observations supply training supervision only.

### B. Encode the prior or the generation process

Representation information may enter earlier than the generated latent endpoint. Two candidate forms are:

- A conditional prior \(u_0=P_\alpha(\epsilon,c(s,g))\), followed by a generator operating from \(u_0\).
- A generator whose intermediate features are aligned with, or conditioned on, CRL representations while its output is an action chunk or an action latent.

Encoding noise by itself supplies no action or goal semantics. A learned prior needs an explicit connection to the action representation through generation, alignment, or action supervision. A learned conditional source distribution also changes the flow-matching training construction. The source samples, target samples, interpolation, and gradient paths must be defined together.

These variants test whether CRL information helps organize the generation process beyond its use as an endpoint target. Compare with a fixed prior under otherwise comparable supervision and compute. Any hidden-feature alignment requires a target and a reason for that feature to carry the relevant action information.

### C. Condition the actor on CRL embeddings

A direct action generator can consume embeddings through

$$
\hat A_H=F_\theta(\epsilon\mid s,g,h_g,h_s),
$$

where \(h_s\) is included only when a suitable state embedding is defined. Compare adding embeddings to raw inputs with replacing selected raw inputs. Replacing raw inputs may discard control information; the comparison should measure this effect.

The same conditioning interface can be used by the latent generator in A or B. This combination tests whether generating in a CRL-informed space and receiving representation conditions have complementary effects. Embedding interventions should check that the actor actually uses the additional information.

### D. Generate an intended future-state embedding

For a fixed prediction interval \(k\), a training condition can be obtained from \(z_{\mathrm{sub}}=\psi(s_{t+k})\). A higher-level generator predicts this condition from available inputs:

$$
\hat z_{\mathrm{sub}}\sim p_\chi(z\mid s,g,k),\qquad
\hat A_H\sim\pi_\theta(A_H\mid s,g,\hat z_{\mathrm{sub}},k).
$$

The low-level actor may generate actions directly or use A's action-latent pathway. The relationship between \(k\), chunk length \(H\), and executed length \(H_{\mathrm{exec}}\) must be explicit. For a chunk-outcome condition, \(k=H\) is a natural candidate. Longer intervals require accounting for behavior beyond the current chunk.

Training on observed future embeddings and deploying on generated embeddings introduces a conditioning distribution shift. An oracle experiment using observed future embeddings can diagnose the low-level actor, but must be labeled separately from deployable performance. Evaluate the generated condition's reachability and usefulness against a final-goal-conditioned actor. Multiple action sequences may reach the same outcome, so an outcome embedding need not identify one unique action chunk.

## 4. Representation learning objectives

### CRL supervision and shared features

The baseline contrastive loss learns to distinguish future goals associated with a state-action chunk from independently sampled goals. Preserve the reference implementation's positive sampling, negative construction, score scaling, and ensemble behavior when reproducing it. A positive future goal is not necessarily the state exactly \(H\) steps after the chunk begins. Fixed-horizon prediction targets and CRL future-goal samples must be distinguished. [Three Steps at a Time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30640)

In a shared architecture, contrastive gradients update \(E\) and \(C\), while action-related losses update the relevant action head and any explicitly shared modules. Projection heads, normalization, similarity scaling, and the actor-facing latent must be stated for each variant. Their usefulness in this setting is an empirical question.

### Action reconstruction or generation supervision

A candidate reconstruction objective is

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rec}}
=\mathbb E\left[\|D(s,R(E(s,A_H)))-A_H\|^2\right].
$$

This encourages action information to survive encoding. It does not establish goal relevance, easy latent generation, or successful control. A stochastic action head may require a distributional objective instead of deterministic MSE, especially when conditioning only on an outcome embedding. State whether the objective supervises the same latent consumed by the actor.

Check whether the decoder uses the latent by replacing or shuffling it while holding state fixed. Measure errors across the chunk, including the executed prefix. A decoder that predicts mainly from state can achieve useful reconstruction statistics while providing little evidence about action information in the latent.

### Action-conditioned JEPA-style prediction

A proposed auxiliary predictor maps current-state information and the action latent to a future-state representation:

$$
\hat h_{t+H}=P_\nu(h_s,z_A,H),\qquad
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pred}}
=\mathbb E\left[\|\hat h_{t+H}
-\operatorname{sg}(\bar\psi(s_{t+H}))\|^2\right].
$$

Here \(\operatorname{sg}\) stops target gradients, and \(\bar\psi\) denotes a frozen or slowly updated target encoder with a compatible input view. A predictor projection can align dimensions. The intended mechanism is to make the action latent informative about action consequences. Action-conditioned latent prediction has precedent in JEPA-based robotic modeling; its benefit for CRAFT's policy extraction remains to be tested. [V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985)

The interval must agree with the action information provided to the predictor. Predicting an arbitrary later state from only the current chunk can require information about unobserved continuation actions. For stochastic outcomes, deterministic regression may average distinct futures.

Compare with state-only prediction and shuffled-action inputs to detect a predictor that ignores the action latent. Specify whether prediction gradients update the state encoder, action encoder, or both. Lower prediction error must be evaluated alongside policy success and action information preservation. Stop-gradient or a moving-average target alone should not be treated as a general guarantee against collapse in the proposed architecture.

### Positive-only objectives and collapse

This branch considers learning positive relationships without explicit negative pairs. Candidate mechanisms include teacher-student prediction and variance/covariance regularization. Pair construction must be defined for control. Current and future states can differ in task-relevant ways, so enforcing invariance across all temporal positives may remove information needed by the actor. An action-conditioned predictor can express a transformation between representations rather than requiring them to coincide. [BYOL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07733), [VICReg](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04906)

Reconstruction places pressure against a constant action latent when the decoder must distinguish different chunks from the same state. It does not guarantee that every shared feature or projection head remains informative. Collapse or loss of action information can occur in an actor-facing projection even if another head supports reconstruction. Monitor per-dimension variance, effective rank, action sensitivity, and the actor's response to conditioning interventions. Interpret global variation together with state-controlled diagnostics, since variation caused only by state can hide weak action information.

Two experimental forms are possible. One keeps the original contrastive critic for scoring while learning an actor-facing representation with a positive-only objective. Another replaces the critic's representation objective itself. Replacing the contrastive objective changes the basis for interpreting its dot product as a reachability-related density ratio. Anti-collapse mechanisms do not restore that interpretation by themselves. A replacement must therefore define and validate the score used for policy improvement. [Contrastive Learning as Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=vGQiU5sqUe3)

### Objective combinations and gradient paths

A schematic representation objective is

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{repr}}
=\lambda_{\mathrm{CRL}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CRL}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{act}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{act}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{pred}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pred}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{reg}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{reg}}.
$$

Each experiment selects a justified subset. The actor's generative and policy improvement objectives are specified separately. Loss weights and gradient sharing can change which information the representation retains. Document detachment, frozen modules, target updates, and whether actor improvement updates the representation. Add auxiliary objectives individually before testing combinations so their effects can be identified.

Inference-time steering is a separate intervention applied to generated candidates or the sampling trajectory. Evaluate its additional success and inference cost independently of representation-learning changes. Related work already studies critic-gradient guidance of flow policies. [Test-Time Gradient Guidance of Flow Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11087)

## 5. Experimental comparisons and interpretation

### Baselines and comparisons

| Comparison | Question answered |
| --- | --- |
| Single-action CRL, CRL+AC across chunk lengths, and DQC | Does the selected setting exhibit the extraction bottleneck, and how effective is the existing shorter-policy remedy? |
| Direct action generation versus a comparable latent generator without CRL representation supervision | Does the latent parameterization itself help? |
| Matched latent generators with and without CRL representation supervision | Does CRL shape a more useful generation space? |
| Raw-input versus embedding-conditioned generation | Does direct representation conditioning help under the same critic feedback? |
| Latent generation, embedding conditioning, and their combination | Are the two pathways complementary? |
| A selected architecture with individual auxiliary objectives | Which representation objective improves downstream control? |

An independent action autoencoder is one possible latent control. Match the action head, latent size, and training protocol as closely as possible. Frozen-encoder experiments can isolate the usefulness of a fixed CRL representation. Joint representation learning needs a separate comparison because it changes both the representation and its training dynamics.

For subgoal conditioning, compare final-goal conditioning, observed-future oracle conditioning, and generated-subgoal conditioning. For positive-only objectives and inference-time steering, compare against the selected main architecture and report the specific additional changes.

### Experimental controls

Use the same dataset splits, goal sampling, evaluation tasks, action bounds, and relevant baseline hyperparameters across comparisons. Hold critic feedback fixed when testing direct access to representation information. For jointly learned variants, record which critic and representation components also change.

Treat critic chunk length, actor output length, and executed length as separate variables. DQC changes actor output length. Fixing the executed length to one action helps diagnose extraction independently of open-loop execution. Subsequently varying execution length tests how much of the generated chunk can be used reliably.

Keep parameter counts, latent dimensions, generation steps, training budgets, and tuning effort comparable, or report their differences. Split trajectories before constructing overlapping chunks to prevent leakage. Handle episode boundaries and incomplete chunks consistently with the baseline. Use multiple seeds and report variability for performance claims. Exact environments, run counts, and compute budgets require experiment-specific definitions.

### Metrics and failure interpretation

- **Control performance.** Report task success, learning curves, and performance versus chunk and execution lengths. Use time-at-goal where the benchmark protocol defines it.
- **Representation and critic diagnostics.** Track held-out discrimination, representation variance/effective rank, action-conditioned prediction, and sensitivity to changes in action or conditioning.
- **Generation diagnostics.** Evaluate held-out reconstruction when applicable, errors along the chunk, generated-latent coverage relative to encoded data, and embedding interventions. Low latent distance or reconstruction error alone does not establish useful control.
- **Cost and reproducibility.** Report training and inference cost, sampling steps, resolved configurations, seeds, dependency versions, and code revisions.

If a latent control and the CRL-informed variant improve similarly, the evidence supports the generative parameterization more strongly than CRL-specific representation learning. If an auxiliary objective improves its own metric but reduces success, investigate objective conflict and information loss. If oracle subgoals help while generated subgoals fail, investigate the subgoal generator and conditioning shift. If performance improves only under one-step replanning, limit the claim to policy extraction under that execution protocol.
